YEARLY MEAN, MEDIAN & P90

IMDb scores by year.

The solid green line is the average (mean): add the title ratings for a year and divide by its title count. The dashed orange line is the median: the middle rating after sorting that year’s titles. The blue dash-dot line is the 90th percentile (P90): the score at or below which at least 90% of that year’s titles fall. Every title counts equally, regardless of its vote total. There is no minimum vote count, score cutoff or smoothing.

Separate graphs for 345,326 movies and 139,880 series/miniseries, with matching axes. For series, year means the year the show premiered. Snapshot: 15 October 2026.

Scope and how to read these graphs

The average is calculated separately for each year and format: sum of title IMDb ratings ÷ number of rated titles. The median is the middle rating after sorting that year’s titles; when there are an even number of titles, it is the average of the two middle ratings. P90 uses the nearest-rank method: sort the ratings and take the rating at position ceil(0.9 × title count), counting from 1. There is no interpolation; tied scores can put more than 90% of titles at or below the threshold. All three statistics use the same titles. Ratings are not weighted by vote totals. The 50,000-vote and median-rating rules used for the connection graph do not apply. A gap in the line means that year has no rated titles; no values are filled in.

The full snapshot contains 345,360 rated movies and 139,913 rated series/miniseries. Of these, 34 movies and 33 series have no known release year and cannot contribute to a yearly average. Unrated titles have no score to average. As in our original distribution, this scope covers non-adult IMDb types movie, tvSeries and tvMiniSeries; individual episodes, shorts, TV movies and other title types are excluded.

Years with few rated titles can fluctuate sharply. The CSV downloads include each year’s mean, median, 90th percentile and title count. Recent years are incomplete, especially 2026, and low-vote title ratings can be unstable. These are present-day ratings grouped by release year, not ratings recorded at the time of release.
