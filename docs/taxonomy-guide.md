# Interpretation notes for the first vocabulary

The vocabulary in `analysis/taxonomy.json` contains descriptive tags, not measured scales. Multiple tags may coexist. These distinctions help avoid misleading connections.

## World rules

| Position | Intended meaning |
| --- | --- |
| grounded-realism | Events operate within ordinary physical and social reality, even when photographed or narrated subjectively. |
| consistent-speculation | A speculative premise has rules that the story substantially treats as consistent: alien biology, future societies or another defined departure. |
| technological-alteration | An invented technology or technological intervention changes what people can do. This can coexist with consistent speculation. |
| supernatural-intrusion | Ghosts, curses, magic or similar supernatural forces intrude into a recognizable ordinary world. Alien visitors and advanced machines belong under speculative or technological rules. |
| mythic-fantasy | Mythic beings, magic or a cosmology form part of how the world operates. |
| heightened-reality | Events or behavior are exaggerated for action, comedy, melodrama or stylization, without requiring a literal supernatural explanation. |
| dream-logic | Associations, transformations or discontinuities operate substantially like dreams. An unreliable memory alone does not establish dream logic. |
| unexplained-phenomena | A consequential departure from ordinary reality remains substantially unexplained. It does not mean ordinary unanswered plot questions. |
| magical-realism | Extraordinary elements are integrated into everyday experience in a way central to the work's approach. |
| documentary-reality | The material presents actual people, events or observations as documentary material. A fiction based on real events is not documentary reality. |
| metafiction | The work meaningfully exposes or plays with its own fiction, representation or construction. A genre homage or familiar trope alone is insufficient. |

An uncanny atmosphere, religious character or surreal image does not by itself establish supernatural events. A subjective film can retain grounded world rules. Different readings may remain provisional or unknown.

## World and setting

Historical society describes the period depicted, not how old the film is. Domestic life, professional workplaces, criminal economies, military societies and detailed institutions describe the social environment organizing the story. They can coexist with urban life or a small community. A story having a small cast does not automatically make its world a small community.

The current world vocabulary is less suitable for nature documentaries than for human fiction. Unknown positions can therefore reflect a vocabulary limitation as well as insufficient knowledge. Future revisions can expand the vocabulary without pretending a nature documentary has human character arcs or a moral attitude toward predators.

## Character, outlook and craft

- Character positions describe major character mechanisms. Worldview describes the work's treatment of its material; an immoral protagonist does not establish an immoral or cynical outlook.
- Tone and emotional progression require an interpretation of the viewing experience. Plot events alone do not determine them.
- Technical style, sound, pacing and endings often need richer material or familiarity with the exact version. Credits, awards, budgets and genre labels do not establish these positions.
- “Minimal” is an assessment of a mechanism's limited role. Unknown is an absence of an adequate assessment. They are different.
- Season and adaptation differences are real limitations of this overall-title first pass.
